Zero bounces against 205 sent across four send days, the highest weekly volume since the domain work began. The lifetime figure eased from 2.1 to 1.9 percent across 2,472 sends with the same 47 addresses retired in total, so nothing new was lost this week. The dead domain count held at 21, meaning no further domain stopped resolving before a message was built for it. The 47 retired records still sit mainly in the stronger tiers, 21 in Tier 3, 20 in Tier 2, 5 in Tier 1 and 1 in Tier 4, which reflects the age of those records rather than the sending. One note on denominators, because the scoreboard below reads differently: 1.9 percent is bounces over every message ever sent, follow ups included, while the scoreboard counts bounces over first touches on each list, which is why the full lead base reads 5.6 percent there.

Done this week
01

Two proof points were pulled out of the copy

Your side checked the partnerships copy against what Ability can actually stand behind, and two claims that had been in use since July did not hold: a named client reference and a figure on time savings across production clients. Both are now rejected at generation, flagged by the automated review that reads every message before it goes out, and the pending queue was re drafted under a new copy version. One replacement proof point, confirmed by your side, is now in use and stated at industry level only, never with a client name. Messages already sent carrying the old claims cannot be pulled back. That is the cost of having run them without checking them first, and it is the reason the check now sits inside the generator rather than in anyone's memory.

02

Subject lines are fixed across the three steps

Each step now carries a set subject rather than a generated one, with the company name cleaned before it goes in and a neutral fallback when the record is too messy to use. Follow ups go out as new messages rather than as replies on top of the original thread, so each step is read on its own subject rather than on a thread the recipient already ignored. Reply detection does not depend on the thread, it matches on sender, so nothing is lost by breaking it.

03

First touches now require a verification stamp

The queue builder refuses to put a first touch on an address that does not carry one, enforced at every point where the queue is assembled rather than by convention. Follow ups are exempt, since the first message already proved the address. Existing rows were stamped from the verification runs already completed, so the rule took effect without stalling the queue. This is what held the week at zero bounces while volume rose to 205.

04

One person per company is in play at a time

A campaign lead is now held while anyone else at the same domain has replied, has opted out, or has a live sequence running. Shared consumer domains are ignored for this. It implements the company level rule your side asked for, and it removes the case where two people at the same firm take a cold opener inside the same two weeks.

05

The sequence has a defined end state

Three emails, then four months of rest counted from the last message, instead of records quietly circling back into the queue. Leads that had already taken three or more messages were moved to rest in one pass, which is part of why the queue this week ran on campaign records rather than on the pool. The single message at the four month mark is the piece still to build; the earliest of those records comes due in November.

06

The scoreboard is live

Per campaign and per wave metrics now come out of one calculation that both writes the weekly file to your repository and fills the table below, so the file and the report cannot disagree. A counting fault went out with it: the gate clock had been reading from the last message of any type, follow ups included, which pushed the reading later every time a follow up landed. It now reads from the last first touch, and the wave 1 gate is pinned to 25 September as agreed.

07

Four replies, after four weeks at zero

One positive, one neutral and two negative. The table below shows the neutral and the negative sitting on wave 2, the first reply signal that campaign has produced. All time the program now stands at 23 replies with 4 positive. The counts are small enough that no rate should be read off a single week; what changed is that the silent stretch ended while the fresh cohorts were in sequence.

Binding constraint: positive replies per completed sequence, not supply and not capacity.
