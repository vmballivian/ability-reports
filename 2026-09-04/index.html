Zero bounces against 92 sent. The lifetime figure eased from 2.3 to 2.2 percent across 2,121 sends, with the same 47 addresses retired in total, so nothing new was lost this week. Eleven more domains stopped resolving and were dropped before a message was ever built for them, taking that count from 8 to 19. The retired records still sit mostly in the stronger tiers, 21 in Tier 3, 20 in Tier 2, 5 in Tier 1 and 1 in Tier 4, which reflects the age of those records rather than the sending. This week the more useful deliverability reading is not the bounce line at all but inbox placement, which is now measured directly and is covered below.

Done this week
01

Inbox placement measured directly instead of inferred

On Tuesday the same message went out in the same minute from all four sending domains to a control inbox, so placement could be read side by side with sending style held constant. The three newer domains landed in the primary tab after four days of live ramp. The original domain still lands in spam after roughly a week out of rotation. Bounce rate says nothing about which folder a message reaches, so this test becomes a standing check rather than a one time diagnostic.

02

Sending moved onto the newer domains while the original one recovers

Nine mailboxes now exist across four domains. Six are in rotation and the three on the original domain stay paused until a placement test puts them back in the inbox. Recovery on a domain that has been landing in spam is measured in weeks and is the expected shape of it, so the pause holds rather than being cut short to protect a volume number.

03

Daily capacity is 29 by design, not by fault

Capacity reads 29 a day rather than 90 because the mailboxes carrying the sending are on a ramp and the paused ones are out. Volume climbed through the week accordingly: 14 on Friday, 13 on Monday, then 20, 23 and 22. That climb is the ramp doing its job. Pushing the newer domains past it to replace the paused volume is exactly how the original domain ended up where it is.

04

92 sent across five send days, all on the new copy

First full week where every first touch and every follow up was written under the recipe that opens on the operational pain of the lead's vertical. Nine messages had gone out on it before this week, so 92 is the first stretch with any body to it. Zero replies. On this denominator that is not a read on the copy, it is a week of sending at a fifth of the usual pace while the placement question gets answered.

05

Follow ups from the paused domain held rather than reassigned

Threads that were opened from the original domain wait for that domain to come back rather than resuming from a different address. Answering an existing thread from a new sender reads as a different person to the recipient and to the receiving filters, so a few days of delay is the cheaper option.

06

Base grew by 149 records, all of it in the lowest tier

The lead base moved from 1,400 to 1,549. Tier 1 held at 29, Tier 2 at 218 and Tier 3 at 627, all unchanged, while Tier 4 went from 526 to 675. Everything that arrived passed domain verification and priority scoring on the way in, so the records are usable, but this is the fourth week running where the growth sits entirely at the bottom of the fit ranking.
