02

The held catch all addresses were resolved by a probe, not an assumption

A block of addresses that no verification service can resolve individually had been sitting out of the queue since the verification rule went in. Rather than guess at them or drop them, a small batch went out from a single mailbox to the strongest of them, inside the normal daily flow. Nothing bounced, and the rest were released on that result. Wave 1c is the visible consequence: 89 imported and 86 first touches already out on a list that was frozen last week.

03

Two new lists cleared copy review before a single message went out

67 consultancy and systems integrator contacts on one list, 41 operations hiring contacts on the other, both read line by line on your side. The wording that came back approved is now stored against each record and the queue sends it verbatim instead of regenerating it, so what was signed off is what leaves. Wave 3 also carries a different angle per partner type rather than one angle stretched across the list.

04

List verification now runs without being asked

When a new or changed list appears in the shared folder, the daily run picks it up, reuses any check under thirty days old, verifies the remainder, and writes the result back with a short note. A cap per list holds anything larger than the limit until it is released by hand, so no large list gets processed without a decision behind it. This is what kept the week at 0.5 percent while three lists entered the program at once.

05

Openers are built from a checkable fact about the company

Your side added a facts column to the lists, one or two verifiable things about each company with a source. Those now land in the record, the opening line is written from them rather than from the segment, and the automated review checks the message against the fact instead of trusting it. A generic opener on a well matched list was the quiet failure mode here, and naming the wrong thing is worse than naming nothing.

06

The partnership claim was corrected to what your side can stand behind

The economics in the partner message were phrased the wrong way round. It now reads that the platform runs the agents, the client owns the install, and the agency earns every month. The automated review flags the inverted version and the corrected phrasing is required on the first touch and the second step, so the old wording cannot return through a regeneration.

07

One seed per active domain went to a monitored inbox on 24 September from within the normal flow, behind real sends and carrying real copy. Placement read off a burst of near identical test messages says something different from placement read off the traffic that actually matters. The result is in the deliverability section above, and it is the reason byabilityai.com is now frozen.

The constraint this week

Binding constraint: sending capacity that stays in the inbox. The fastest ramp reached full volume and landed in spam, so capacity now comes only from the two slower arms. Lead supply is not binding, and the reply side is an ICP question still being worked with your side.
Evidence: on send day 21 the 20 day arm, byabilityai.com, seeded to spam while the 40 and 60 day arms seeded to primary; byabilityai.com had carried 251 of 375 messages this week, 66.9 percent, and 40 of the 81 wave 1 first emails; with it frozen, daily capacity moves from 119 to 40.
Next lever: the 40 and 60 day arms keep their planned ramps instead of being pushed to cover the gap. The number that shows it worked is their seeds staying in primary as they pass 10 a day per mailbox.
If it doesn't: the safe ceiling per new domain is lower than the ramp assumes, and growth has to come from slower ramps across more domains rather than more volume per domain.
