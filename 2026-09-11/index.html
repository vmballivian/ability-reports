Zero bounces against 146 sent, on a week where volume rose against the last one and where every address from the new lists was net new to the sending domains. The lifetime figure eased from 2.2 to 2.1 percent across 2,267 sends, with the same 47 addresses retired in total, so nothing new was lost. Two more domains stopped resolving and were dropped before a message was ever built for them, taking that count from 19 to 21. The 47 retired records still sit mainly in the stronger tiers, 21 in Tier 3, 20 in Tier 2, 5 in Tier 1 and 1 in Tier 4, which reflects the age of those records rather than the sending. Clean bounce numbers on a week of net new addresses are the point of putting verification in front of the queue instead of behind it.

Done this week
01

Wave 1 of the partnerships campaign moved through the queue

All 81 verified addresses from the first list are in the base and in sequence. The first 27 went out on Friday and the rest followed on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday as the ramp allowed. These go out on the partner angle, that is, what these firms ship to their own clients, rather than on the vertical pain copy the main program runs. They also sit ahead of everything else in the daily queue, so the cohort moves as one block and reads as one cohort instead of being smeared across three weeks.

02

The follow on lists cleared verification and 129 more records came in

The fallback list and the second wave went through the same two step check as wave 1, domain resolution first and then mailbox verification. 129 addresses came back clean and are now in the base, 35 from the fallback list and 94 from wave 2. Nothing enters the queue without passing that check plus suppression against every address already in the CRM and every domain that has opted out, so this is genuinely additive rather than a second pass over names the program has already touched.

03

Base growth landed at the top of the fit ranking for the first time

The base moved from 1,549 to 1,783. Tier 1 went from 29 to 139 and Tier 2 from 218 to 318, while Tier 3 held flat at 627 and Tier 4 added 24 to reach 699. Of the 234 records added, 210 sit in the top two tiers, one contact per company. For four straight weeks the growth had been entirely in the lowest tier, which is the pattern that gets reported here every week until it changes. This week it changed.

04

Daily capacity rose from 29 to 49

The six live mailboxes advanced on their ramp, so capacity is now 49 a day against 29 last week. The three mailboxes on the original domain stay out of rotation. Daily volume tracked the ramp rather than jumping ahead of it: 29 on Friday, 33 on Monday, 40 on Tuesday and 44 on Thursday. Wednesday had no send.

05

Zero replies, and the fourth consecutive week at zero

That is the flat reading and it is worth stating plainly. The last four weeks ran on a base whose top two tiers had not moved since July and on volume cut to a fifth while the domain question was settled. Both of those changed this week. The wave 1 cohort is the first genuinely fresh, top tier, one per company list the program has sent into, and its first touches only landed across the last four send days, which is inside the window where a reply would not be expected yet. The read on it comes next week, not from this number.

06

Every reply is now classified as it arrives

Replies are labeled on arrival against the definitions your side set: positive, neutral, negative, wrong person, opt out, auto reply and bounce. Automated labels are reviewed by hand, and once a label is set by hand no later run overwrites it. The history was backfilled, so 14 of the 19 replies the program has taken to date carry an outcome and the remaining 5 predate the change. This is what makes reply quality readable per campaign instead of one number for the whole program, which matters now that two campaigns run side by side.

07

93 catch all addresses held rather than sent

Those 93 sit on domains that accept any address, so no verifier can confirm them and a send is the only test left. They are held, not dropped. The open option is a small probe from a single secondary mailbox, reading the bounce rate before releasing the rest. It waits on the wave 1 reading, because three bounces in one day pauses a mailbox for the day and that cost lands on capacity that is still ramping.
