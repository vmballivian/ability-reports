The constraint this week

Binding constraint: eligible records on the day, not the daily allowance. The allowance rose from 79 to 93 and the queue only filled because lists that were sitting on hold were released into it.
Evidence: 408 sent at 92.9 percent of the allowance across five send days, at 67, 83, 73, 91 and 94. Almost all of the week's new first touches came from lists released this week: ops hiring wave 2 at 91 imported and 66 first touches, the international partnership waves at 54, 19 and 4, and the account based rounds at 11, 3 and 41 imported. 769 records sit queued and most are not available on a given day, because only one person per company is contacted per day, a first touch waits for address verification, and the rolling pool is paused on its bounce level.
Next lever: the sources that are left, which are the international records parked for having no partner angle, a probe of the held catch all addresses, and records in the older base last contacted more than 30 days ago, plus further account research rounds on a wider filter. The number that would show it worked is five send days at the full allowance without another held list being released to reach it, with bounce rate under the 5 percent ceiling and every sending domain's seed in primary.
If it doesn't: the allowance stops being the ceiling and supply sets the volume, so weekly sending tracks how fast verified records arrive rather than how fast warmup climbs, and that is a list question rather than an infrastructure one.
